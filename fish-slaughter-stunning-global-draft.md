# How can pain and distress at slaughter be reduced for farmed fish? — draft, 2026-10-11

**Status:** draft for source review. **One-line summary:** Most farmed fish worldwide are still killed without stunning, via methods that cause prolonged distress (asphyxia in air or ice, CO₂ narcosis, gill cutting without stunning); electrical and percussive stunning can render fish insensible quickly and are increasingly adopted for some species, but effective implementation is species-specific and verification of stun efficacy remains a major unresolved problem.

## Scope
- Species/population and production context: farmed finfish across species groups (salmonids, carp, sea bass/sea bream, tilapia, eel, catfish); harvested for human consumption. Fish are farmed in very diverse systems (sea cages, ponds, tanks, raceways, ponds drained for harvest).
- Geography and time horizon: global, with emphasis on EU/UK where statutory and review evidence is strongest; the note is current as of 2026-10-11.
- Relevant lifetime window: pre-slaughter handling (fasting, crowding, pumping, transport) and the killing process itself; minutes to hours, experienced by every fish harvested.
- Decision-maker and decision: producers, retailers and certifiers choosing harvest and stunning methods; whether to require pre-slaughter stunning and to verify it.

## Proposed causal pathway
Fish are sentient and capable of experiencing pain, fear and stress (consensus reviews cited below). Killing methods such as asphyxia in air or ice slurry, CO₂ narcosis, and gill cutting without prior stunning leave the fish conscious while experiencing severe distress over minutes to hours (longer than in mammals because ectotherms lose consciousness slowly). Pre-stunning (electrical or percussive) is intended to induce immediate, irreversible loss of consciousness until death, thereby removing that experience. The pathway can fail if stunning parameters are wrong for the species, if fish are not correctly positioned (a known failure mode in electrical systems), or if fish recover consciousness before death.

**Scale context (uncertain):** farmed finfish killed annually number in the tens of billions by official FAO tonnage and plausibly far more by individual count (fish-count estimates have wide uncertainty ranges because of heterogeneous species sizes). Every one of these fish passes through pre-slaughter handling and killing, so per-animal severity here is applied to a very large population—though species, system and region strongly condition which methods are actually used. Treat any specific count as an order-of-magnitude estimate pending verified data.

## Expected impact and uncertainty
- Animals affected: all fish harvested by a producer or supply chain that adopts stunning; very large populations per intervention site.
- Magnitude, severity and duration: removal of distress ranging from minutes to hours of severe aversive experience per fish at harvest, the most concentrated welfare insult in the production cycle; basis is time-to-unconsciousness data and expert syntheses above.
- Probability of adoption: moderate in EU salmon/trout sectors; lower where infrastructure is absent (pond harvest, small-scale systems, some species such as eel). Adoption often follows retailer/certifier requirements rather than regulation.
- Counterfactual: if production shifts to regions or systems without stunning (e.g., market substitution away from high-welfare supply chains), the improvement is displaced rather than realised—check where fish go when procurement changes.
- Confidence: moderate that pre-slaughter stunning reduces suffering at killing for major species; low-to-moderate on current global prevalence and on efficacy for less-studied species.
- Sentience / moral-weight assumptions: the note assumes fish experience pain and distress (supported by the cited reviews). Readers weighting fish far below mammals should test whether the conclusion changes: because the affected population is enormous and the insult is severe and universal, this conclusion is relatively robust to wide ranges of relative moral weight.

## Risks, trade-offs, and alternatives
- Electrical stunning can fail silently if fish are misaligned or parameters are wrong; a poorly verified "stunned" process may be no better than no stunning. Verification of insensibility (behavioural and physiological checks) is essential, not optional.
- Pre-slaughter handling itself (crowding, pumping, poor water quality) causes hypoxia, stress and trauma and is not solved by stunning; improvements must not be limited to the kill step.
- Equipment cost may concentrate production; where investment is unaffordable, alternative low-cost improvements (shorter air exposure, immediate percussive spiking/coring by trained staff) should be considered against doing nothing.
- Reducing meat demand or shifting consumption is an alternative lever that avoids slaughter entirely but substitutes other considerations this note does not evaluate.

## Conclusion
For the main farmed species, evidence supports stunning before killing (electrical or percussive, with species-appropriate parameters and verified insensibility) over asphyxia, CO₂ narcosis, live chilling or gill cutting without stunning. It should not be inferred that stunning is universally implemented, that any system labelled "stunning" is effective, or that pre-slaughter handling welfare is covered by the intervention. Evidence that would change the conclusion: robust verification data showing commercial systems reliably render the target species insensible, or new evidence that currently-used methods achieve equivalent loss of consciousness.
