# How can pain from hot-iron disbudding be reduced in dairy calves? — draft, 2026-10-10

**Status:** draft for source review. **Bottom line:** Hot-iron disbudding causes pain, and local anaesthesia (LA) plus a systemic NSAID can reduce measured pain responses across different peri-operative phases. LA alone does not reliably address pain when the block wears off; an NSAID alone does not numb the burn during disbudding. This is not a case for routine disbudding: where feasible, consider polled genetics or management that accommodates horned cattle. The extent and duration of pain relief, especially through wound healing, remain uncertain.

## Scope and decision
For dairy calves undergoing hot-iron disbudding, which pain-mitigation components reduce pain during the procedure and recovery, and what remains painful? Core studies include calves from a few days old through 12 weeks; this spans meaningful age variation, so ages are reported per study rather than pooled as if equivalent. England’s code recommends disbudding before 2 months, ideally when buds first appear. This note concerns pain evidence, not instructions or drug dosing; medicines and veterinary oversight must follow current local rules. Excludes caustic paste and surgical dehorning from the main comparison because they are different procedures with distinct welfare profiles.

## What the evidence says
| Phase / claim | Evidence | Limits and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Disbudding itself is painful; an effective cornual nerve block reduces acute responses, but a block may be incomplete and its effect wanes. | Winder et al. (2018), systematic review/meta-analysis of cautery-disbudding interventions in calves ≤12 weeks; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29550129/), DOI [10.3168/jds.2017-14092](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2017-14092). | LA was associated with reduced cortisol response through about 2 h; studies also showed a later rise around 4 h. Outcomes and measurement times varied. Evidence supports LA for acute pain, not pain-free treatment. Check block efficacy before proceeding. |
| NSAID added to LA can reduce some delayed pain indicators as LA wanes. | Stewart et al. (2009), randomized 6-arm study, 46 Holstein-Friesian calves (mean 33 days; range 30–39 d), hot-iron versus sham and no mitigation / LA / LA + IV meloxicam; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19307632/), DOI [10.3168/jds.2008-1578](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2008-1578). | Without pain relief, heart rate rose ~35 bpm in first 5 min and stayed above baseline for 3 h. In LA-only calves, physiological changes at 2–3 h coincided with block wearing off; adding meloxicam mitigated this onset. HR/HRV/eye temperature are indirect pain indicators; modest sample. |
| NSAID benefit is supported by some behavioral outcomes, but not every measure or study. | Duffield et al. (2010), randomized trial, 40 Holstein heifers aged 4–8 weeks; all received lidocaine block, then ketoprofen or saline 10 min before electric cautery; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20514252/), full text [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2822372/). | Ketoprofen group showed fewer ear flicks/total head behaviors; cortisol did not differ; starter intake only tended higher. These outcomes support an added NSAID effect on some post-procedure behaviors, not a universal effect on all endpoints. |
| Wounds can remain sensitive after the acute peri-operative window, despite common mitigation. | Adcock & Tucker (2018), randomized 48 calves disbudded or sham-disbudded at 3 or 35 days, with LA and meloxicam; pain sensitivity followed through healing; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30146290/), DOI [10.3168/jds.2018-14987](https://doi.org/10.3168/jds.2018-14987). EFSA 2025 summarizes re-epithelialization taking weeks and persistent sensitivity. | This is not a trial comparing analgesia regimens: it contextualizes duration/residual pain. Do not imply a single peri-operative NSAID prevents healing-phase pain. Age effects are not settled. |

EFSA’s 2025 cattle welfare opinion concludes that properly administered LA and post-procedure analgesia reduce pain, but pain can persist for weeks. Cornual blocks can fail through placement, agent, timing or lack of efficacy testing; check desensitization before disbudding. The opinion also notes persistent sensitivity findings and uncertainty around very early-age disbudding. See [EFSA opinion](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2025.9518), sections 3.6.1.3–3.6.1.5. These are evidence synthesis and expert conclusions, not evidence that disbudding is necessary.

## Practical and legal context (England; checked 2026-10-10; not legal advice)
The England Code of Recommendations says disbudding should take place before calves are two months old, ideally when the bud first appears; recommends heated-iron disbudding under local anaesthetic by a trained, competent stock-keeper; and says allow time for the block to take effect and test with a skin prick. It says chemical cauterisation is strongly discouraged. The code describes an anaesthetic-law exception for chemical cauterisation only during the first week of life; this does not establish that paste is a welfare-equivalent method. Consult current legal and veterinary-medicines requirements for the specific nation, method and product. [England cattle code, paras. 117–119](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-cattle/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-cattle).

## Conclusion and uncertainty
Evidence supports using both effective LA and systemic analgesia when hot-iron disbudding is performed: they address different phases. But the studies are heterogeneous, each outcome captures only part of pain, and post-procedure treatment does not establish absence of pain during healing. The 2018 meta-analysis is useful but predates newer studies; an updated synthesis and adequately powered field trials with standardized behavior, wound sensitivity, feeding/activity and healing outcomes would improve protocol comparisons. Report age, agent, timing, block verification and follow-up per study. Do not infer that pain mitigation makes disbudding necessary or harmless; breeding polled cattle and husbandry that permits horned animals are alternatives for consideration.
